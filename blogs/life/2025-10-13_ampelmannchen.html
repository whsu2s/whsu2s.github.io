Meeting the Green Ampelmännchen in Berlin 在柏林與小綠人的相遇

Posted on 13 October, 2025

在德東的城市漫步，除了略顯樸素而整潔的街道，最引人注意的，莫過於紅綠燈上那個戴著小帽子的紅綠燈小人。 這位可愛的指揮官誕生於 1961 年，由畢業於柏林洪堡大學的工程心理學家卡爾・佩格勞（Karl Peglau）設計。 他希望創造一種讓孩童與長者都能一眼理解的交通號誌圖像：綠燈是一位戴帽、邁開步伐往左走的小綠人；紅燈則是一位戴著圓頂帽、雙手平舉立正的小紅人。 根據布萊梅大學的一項研究[1]，佩格勞設計的紅綠燈小人不僅童趣可愛，更是結合了心理學的巧思。 相較於西德的圖像設計，東德的紅綠燈小人更能減少人在辨識顏色與圖形時產生的「斯特魯普效應」（Stroop effect），讓指示更加清晰、直觀。 這些簡單卻生動的圖像，不僅提高了行人安全，也成為至今仍廣受歡迎的東德時期文化象徵。

七年前，我第一次造訪柏林。那時我住宿在柏林西邊一戶烏克蘭人家裡。 屋主的兒子是一位樂手，家裡擺著一台鋼琴。面對一個從外地來的亞洲學生，他毫不猶豫地讓我使用他的琴，並坐下來靜靜地欣賞。 而他的母親雖然不太會說英文或德文，但總以真誠的笑容迎接我，每天早上都替我準備早餐。 即使只有短短幾天，因為他們的善意與陪伴，我似乎更快地適應了異國的生活，也更有動力精進我的琴藝。 如今回想起來，他們就像我人生道路上的小綠人，引領我勇敢向前。

然而，人生並非只有綠燈。我們也會遇到那些讓我們停下腳步的小紅人。 也許是別人的批評、誤點的火車，又或者是一段困頓、一場病，或一個突如其來的別離，令人焦慮甚至是悲傷。 有些人會選擇闖紅燈，有些人則選擇繞路，也有人選擇等待。 等待有時很難熬，因為有很多事等著我們去做去嘗試；但也因為等待，讓我們得以停下腳步反思，重新調整方向。

在歐洲的其他城市，紅綠燈也被賦予在地的文化意涵。 荷蘭烏特勒支（Utrecht）以米飛兔（Nijntje）作為號誌人物，讓行人過街時也能感受到溫馨的童趣。 而柏林的紅綠燈小人，則成為城市的象徵及人生的提醒。 我們都希望在人生的路上遇到那些幫助我們前行的小綠人，無論是一句鼓勵、一個機會，或一段陪伴。 但有時，那些讓我們暫時停下的小紅人也同樣重要。因為正是在停下來的片刻，我們得以整理思緒、恢復力量，再一次踏上旅程。

When strolling through cities in former East Germany, beyond the modest yet orderly streets, what catches the eye most is the Ampelmännchen, a little traffic light figure wearing a hat. This charming “commander” was born in 1961, designed by Karl Peglau, an engineering psychologist who graduated from Humboldt University of Berlin. Peglau sought to create traffic symbols that could be easily understood by both children and the elderly: the green light features a small figure in a hat striding confidently to the left, while the red light shows a figure in a rounded cap standing upright with arms outstretched. According to a study from the University of Bremen [1], Peglau’s design was not only playful and endearing but also grounded in psychological insight. Compared with the West German version, the East German “Ampelmännchen” reduces the cognitive interference between color and shape recognition, a phenomenon known as the Stroop effect, making the signals clearer and more intuitive. These simple yet expressive figures not only enhanced pedestrian safety but have also become an enduring cultural icon of East Germany.

Seven years ago, I visited Berlin for the first time. I stayed with a Ukrainian family in the western part of the city. The son, a musician, kept a piano in their living room. When he learned that I was an Asian student from abroad, he didn’t hesitate to let me play it, sitting quietly nearby to listen. His mother, who spoke little English or German, greeted me with a sincere smile and prepared breakfast for me every morning. Though I only stayed for a few days, their kindness and company helped me adapt more easily to life in a foreign country, and inspired me to practice the piano with greater dedication. Looking back now, they were like the little green man at the crosswalk, guiding me forward with quiet encouragement.

However, life is not made up of green lights alone. We all encounter those little red figures that make us stop in our tracks. They may come in the form of someone’s criticism, a delayed train, a period of hardship or illness, or an unexpected farewell that fills us with anxiety or sorrow. Some people choose to run the red light; others take a detour; still others simply wait. Waiting can be difficult, as there is always so much to do, so much to try, but it is through waiting that we pause to reflect and find the chance to readjust our direction.

In other European cities, traffic lights have also been imbued with local cultural meaning. In Utrecht, the Netherlands, Miffy (Nijntje) serves as the signal figure, adding a touch of warmth and childhood charm to the simple act of crossing the street. In Berlin, the Ampelmännchen has become not only a symbol of the city but also a gentle reminder about life itself. We all hope to meet those little green figures who help us move forward, whether through a word of encouragement, a new opportunity, or a moment of companionship. Yet sometimes, the little red figures that make us pause are just as important. It is in those moments of stillness that we gather our thoughts, regain our strength, and prepare to set out once more.
