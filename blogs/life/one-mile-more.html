多走一里路 Go the Extra Mile

Posted on 20 July, 2025

「有人強逼你走一里路，你就同他走二里。」（馬太5:41）
"And if anyone forces you to go one mile, go with him two miles." (Matthew 5:41)

在德國很容易享受步行的樂趣。無論春夏秋冬，走在市中心的徒步街道、森林小徑或廣闊田野間，總能安心地欣賞風景，在漫步中沉澱思緒。

最近讀到馬太福音5:41 [1]，有了一些新的體會。 耶穌所處的時代，猶太人正受羅馬統治，羅馬士兵有權強迫百姓幫忙背重物，「走一里路」成了一種不得不履行的義務。 今天的我們，也都有自己的「一里路」：可能是公民的義務、工作的本分、或人際關係中的壓力與期待。這些事看似瑣碎，有時甚至令人煩躁，卻常是鍛鍊心志與塑造品格的過程。 那麼，為什麼耶穌鼓勵我們在完成這些責任之後，還要「多走一里路」呢？ 這第二里路不再是被迫，而是出於選擇。它可能是多一點思考、多一份付出、或主動走出舒適圈以成就比自身利益更大的事。 這份自願，讓行動不再只是應付，而成為生命的擴展。真正的自由，或許就在這多走的一里之中展現出來。

經文的這段話讓我在散步或做事時有了不太一樣的態度。 走過一段覺得該停下來的路，若願意再多走一些，常會遇見意想不到的風景與感動。 生命中的「第二里路」，也許就是這樣悄悄開啟新的視野的地方。

In Germany, walking is something to be enjoyed. Whether it’s spring, summer, autumn, or winter, whether you’re in a pedestrian zone downtown, a forest trail, or a quiet countryside path, there’s always a sense of peace in taking in the scenery and thinking as you walk.

Recently, a verse from the Bible (Matthew 5:41) gave me new insight. In Jesus’ time, the Jewish people lived under Roman rule, and Roman soldiers had the legal right to compel civilians to carry their burdens for one mile. That first mile represented duty — a social obligation, a legal requirement, perhaps even a relational burden. These “must-do” tasks can sometimes feel meaningless, but often they’re where our character and capacity are shaped. But why go the second mile after that? This next stretch isn’t required — it’s a choice. It may mean doing a little more, thinking a little deeper, or willingly giving something up. Unlike the first mile, walked out of obligation, the second is an act of freedom. It doesn’t just strengthen what we can do; it broadens who we are.

And perhaps that’s what I’ve found during these long walks through every season — that some of the most meaningful growth doesn’t come in the miles we have to walk, but in the quiet, chosen steps we take beyond them.
