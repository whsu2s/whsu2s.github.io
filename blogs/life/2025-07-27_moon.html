外國的月亮比較亮 When the Moon Shines Brighter

Posted on 30 July, 2025

「人有悲歡離合，月有陰晴圓缺，此事古難全。但願人長久，千里共嬋娟。」蘇軾《水調歌頭》

從小我就喜歡抬頭看月亮，觀看那似嫦娥又似玉兔的陰影。到了國外，我甚至更常抬頭望月。 幾個夜晚，滿月明亮得將我從夢中喚醒，窗外灑滿銀光，彷彿邀請我和自己的影子來場寧靜的夜聚。 我曾和朋友在晚飯後邊散步邊賞月，讓它默默看著我們談笑風生。 也曾在孤單的夜裡，一個人望著月亮緩緩升起，心裡的迷惘與寂寞也被那溫柔的光照亮了一角。

「人有悲歡離合，月有陰晴圓缺。」人際關係如同月亮的盈虧，有時圓滿，有時缺憾，總讓人心中百味雜陳。 英國人類學家Robin Dunbar在1990年代提出「鄧巴數」（Dunbar's number），指出人類的社交圈存在上限，大約是 150 人，而真正親密的好友只有 5 位左右。 回想在德國的日子，我曾與來自三十六個國家的人相識，總數或許剛好接近這個數字；幸運的是，我擁有超過五位能深入交流的朋友。 那些朋友中雖然有的已經不再聯絡，但是他們為我的生命帶來了不同的色彩。

有人說「外國的月亮比較圓」，而我的經驗是「外國的月亮比較亮」。 在德國，少了些污染與光害，也多了些靜謐與與世界各地朋友交流的機會。 皎潔的月光更真切地映照心靈深處，使我在思念與孤寂之間，生出對生命的反思與盼望。 月亮有盈虧，友情有聚散，生命的某些時刻或許如朔月般幽暗，但詩人提醒我，那些黯淡的日子只是生命的一部分，也終將過去。 無論順境、逆境或平凡的日子裡，多多思想並抱著盼望，是一種美好的力量。

七月三十日是國際友誼日。願我們都能珍惜身邊的朋友，讓友誼長久，如月光般溫柔而美好。

Since childhood, I love looking up at the moon, gazing at the shadows that resemble Chang’e and the Jade Rabbit. When I went abroad, I found myself lifting my eyes to the moon even more often. On several nights, the full moon was so bright that it woke me from my dreams, its silver light spilling through the window as if inviting me and my shadow to a quiet night gathering. I have walked with friends after dinner, chatting and laughing under the moonlight as it silently watched over us. I have also sat alone on tranquil nights, watching the moon slowly rise, while its gentle light illuminated a corner of my heart filled with confusion and loneliness.

“People have sorrow and joy; the moon has its waxing and waning.” Relationships, like the phases of the moon, are sometimes full, sometimes broken, and always stir up a mixture of emotions. In the 1990s, British anthropologist Robin Dunbar proposed the “Dunbar’s number,” suggesting that humans can only maintain stable social relationships with about 150 people, with truly close friends numbering only around five. Looking back on my days in Germany, I met people from thirty-six different countries, and the total number may have been close to this limit. Fortunately, I had more than five friends with whom I shared genuine, deep connections. Some of them I no longer keep in touch with, but they have each brought a unique color to my life.

There is a saying that “the moon is rounder in foreign lands,” but my experience is that “the moon abroad is brighter.” In Germany, with less pollution and light interference, and a quieter pace of life filled with opportunities to connect with people from around the world, the moonlight seemed to shine more clearly into the depths of my heart. It gave me space to reflect on life, to find hope even in moments of longing and solitude. The moon waxes and wanes; friendships come together and drift apart. At times, life may feel as dark as a new moon, but as the poet reminds us, those dim days are only a part of the journey and will eventually pass. Whether in times of joy, hardship, or ordinary days, I am reminded of the words: "In the day of prosperity be joyful, and in the day of adversity consider,“ which reminds me to embrace each season of life with hope and thoughtfulness.

July 30th marks International Friendship Day. May we all cherish the friends around us, letting our friendships endure gently and beautifully, like the moonlight.
