Efficient HER2 Prediction Using Cell Templates and Pericellular DAB Intensity

Posted on 12 July, 2025

HER2 (Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2) is a critical biomarker in breast cancer pathology, guiding decisions for targeted therapies. HER2 status is typically assessed via immunohistochemical (IHC) staining and scored on a scale from 0 to 3+ (Table 1), based on the proportion and intensity of membrane staining in tumor cells. A major challenge in clinical settings is the subjectivity of visual scoring, especially for borderline cases between 0 and 1+, which significantly affects treatment eligibility. Deep learning (DL)-based approaches have shown promise but often struggle to distinguish these subtle categories due to limitations in accurately detecting overlapping or faintly stained tumor cells [1]. Instead of relying solely on data-hungry black-box models, we proposed a classical computer vision pipeline to more precisely detect and quantify HER2 status between HER2 score 0 and 1+.

Table 1: HER2 Scoring Guidelines [2]
HER2 Score Assessment Staining Pattern
0 Negative No staining or faint/barely perceptible membrane staining in less than 10% of tumor cells
1+ Negative Barely perceptible incomplete membrane staining in greater than 10% of tumor cells
2+ Equivocal A weak to moderate complete membrane staining is observed in > 10% of tumor cells
3+ Positive Strong (intense and uniform) complete membrane staining is observed in > 10% of invasive tumor cells

Our pipeline specifically targets challenging HER2 0 vs 1+ classification cases. We first perform color deconvolution to extract both the DAB channel (for HER2 membrane staining) and the hematoxylin channel (for nuclei). Template matching is used to detect individual cell centroids, even in crowded regions. We then generate a binary mask from the hematoxylin channel to identify candidate cell regions and filter false positives. For each remaining cell, we measure the DAB intensity in a thin ring around the nucleus. A cell is marked HER2-positive if the stained fraction exceeds a threshold, and the final HER2 percentage is computed as the ratio of positive cells to all detected tumor cells.

To support practical usage, we developed a lightweight GUI tool that allows users to interactively select regions of interest (ROI) on the whole-slide image. Upon selection, the tool extracts the ROI, performs HER2 quantification, and visualizes both the original and DAB-stained regions side by side. The entire process, from ROI selection to HER2 score estimation, takes under 30 seconds, making it feasible for routine clinical or research workflows.
